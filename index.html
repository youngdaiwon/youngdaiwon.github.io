I am an Assistant Professor of Marketing at the Charles F. Dolan School of Business, Fairfield University. My research examines consumer decision making and the psychological processes that shape how people evaluate products, information, and emerging technologies. I am particularly interested in topics at the intersection of consumer behavior, digital marketing, language, technology, and cognitive psychology. Much of my work uses experimental methods to identify the mechanisms underlying consumer judgments, preferences, and behavior.

Publications

Price Promotions: A Review and Peek into the Future

Hongjun Ye, Jintao Zhang, Youngdai Won, and Rajneesh Suri

Encyclopedia of Pricing, Elgar Publishing, 203–206, 2024

Abstract
Price promotions serve as integral tools for businesses, facilitating sales enhancement and brand engagement. This comprehensive review explores diverse strategies within price promotions, encompassing tactics such as price comparison, framing, sensory influences, and processing differences, elucidating their impact on consumer behavior. Delving into promotion design, it highlights the significance of price presentation, product display, and monetary versus non-monetary promotions, underscoring their varied influences on consumer responses. Additionally, it introduces the Input-Design-Output-Adjust (IDOA) model, emphasizing the iterative nature of price promotion strategies, leveraging consumer insights and technological advancements for adaptive implementations. This model advocates for adaptable approaches to adjust and refine promotions in real time, crucial in today's dynamic market landscape. With extensive references to empirical studies, the article outlines a comprehensive framework to navigate the complexities and harness the potential of price promotions.

Suffix of Morpheme Transposition Effect in Korean Word Recognition

Youngdai Won and Chang Hoan Lee

Social Science Studies, 30(1), 132–158, 2022

Abstract
In this study, two experiments were conducted to investigate the role of suffix in a Korean word, focusing on the position flexibility of suffix. Experiment 1, using the single stimulus presentation method, compared the performance of nonwords with suffixes with their control nonwords in order to examine the role of the morpheme and positional specificity. The results showed that the nonword with suffix and the suffix-transposed nonword were processed more slowly than their control nonwords. This pattern indicates that the suffix is decomposed in orthographic processing and that its location is flexible, partially activating the original word. Experiment 2, using a masked priming task, found that the suffix-transposed nonword did not facilitate processing of the target more than the non-suffix-transposed nonword in reaction time, but the former reduced error rates more than the latter. This pattern suggests that the location of the transposed suffix is flexible, eventually enhancing performance for the corresponding target. Overall, this study provides evidence for the role of suffixes and flexibility of suffix positioning in Korean orthographic processing.

Cyclical Consumption and Expected Stock Returns: Evidence from the Korean Capital Market

Peter Y. Won, Chaehwan Won, and Youngdai Won

Global Business and Finance Review, 26(3), 14–32, 2021

Abstract
This study empirically demonstrates how the new variable of cyclical consumption can capture consumption risk and predict expected stock returns, examines whether the relationship is stronger for KOSPI or KOSDAQ, and identifies industries exhibiting stronger or weaker relationships with cyclical consumption in the Korean capital market. The analysis uses return predictive regressions, alternative detrending methods, an external habit model, and related approaches. Cyclical consumption has a statistically significant inverse relationship with market returns, and the relationship becomes stronger as market returns are accumulated over longer horizons. The relationship is stronger for KOSPI than KOSDAQ and is also observed across a range of KOSPI industry portfolios. The findings provide implications for investors, policymakers, and other market participants and offer evidence on cyclical consumption as a predictor of stock returns in Korea.

The Stem Transposition Effects in Korean Word Recognition

Youngdai Won and Chang Hoan Lee

Journal of Language Sciences, 26(2), 253–271, 2019

Abstract
The present study focused on how morphemes are coded for positions in the word recognition system. The experiment investigated whether stem morphemes are sensitive to morpheme positional distribution. Masked priming strategies were used to observe facilitation effects. Compound words were selected as targets, while the conditions comprised three different stimuli. The results showed that transposed morpheme conditions facilitate the identification of target words, indicating that stem representations are flexible in position coding. The syllable transposition effect is comparable to the absence of a syllable transposition effect in previous studies. Further research using longer multisyllabic words is needed.

Laughter: Psychological Mechanisms, Considerations, Future Directions

Chang Hoan Lee, Youngdai Won, Jungyul Park, and Jeongsub Lim

Journal of Brain Behaviour and Cognitive Sciences, 2(1), 1–5, 2018

Abstract
Psychological research on laughter has become an important trend in emotional psychology. Major previous studies and theories are reviewed with a focus on the positive effects and cognitive mechanisms of laughter. Several limitations of previous studies and related considerations are discussed. As future directions for research on laughter, more elaborated topics are suggested, including AI-based emotional detection.
